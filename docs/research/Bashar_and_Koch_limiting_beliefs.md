# Five new cores complete the belief tree

Taken together, Bashar's teachings and Richard Koch's books add **170 new surface beliefs and 5 new cores** to The Belief Lab. That takes the tree from 32 cores and 351 surface beliefs to **37 cores and 521 surface beliefs**, with every new belief under exactly one core. The research notes held about 246 candidate proposals and several hundred raw passages. Most of what Bashar names is already in v0.5: deservability, "no pain, no gain", outcome attachment, scarcity, purpose and "following excitement is irresponsible". What was missing falls into five areas. The first is a group of *meta-beliefs* about the mind itself: needing certainty before moving, and treating thoughts and feelings as facts. The second is grief held as loyalty. The third is receiving framed as debt. The fourth, and Koch's main contribution, is the belief that results scale with effort. The fifth is a long tail of past-determinism and "events have built-in meaning" beliefs that fit existing cores. The core decisions are: adopt `certain`, adopt a merged `fused` (absorbing the proposed `fixed`), adopt `grief`, adopt `owe`, and adopt `grind`. Do not adopt `struggle`. Its "efficacy" half goes into `grind`, and its "worth" and "permission" halves stay in `earn`, `deserve` and `joy`. These cores matter because two of them, `certain` and `fused`, describe exactly where the book's Formula breaks down for a reader. "Act on the pull without insisting on the outcome" fails for someone who must be sure first, or who reads every fear as a fact. Three safety lines run through the material: grief copy must stay gentle, Bashar's "you create your victimhood" framing must never reach the app, and anhedonia and permanent-low-mood items need a route to help.

## Bashar names eight recurring clusters, and v0.5 already holds half of them

The sources differ more than their titles suggest. The four project PDFs are **four consecutive chunks of one book**, *Bashar: Blueprint for Change* (pp. i–302), not four volumes. Most of the belief content sits in chapters 1 and 6–9 (pp. 3–176). The later chapters are mostly extraterrestrial material, so their beliefs are mostly implied. *The Masters of Limitation* (2020) describes in detail how beliefs disguise and reinforce themselves, but it gives almost no concrete examples. Its copyright page labels it "a work of fiction" (Masters, front matter). Fetchable online material is almost all secondary, such as Formula summaries and quote collections ([Transcend with Celine](https://transcendwithceline.com/methods/bashar-the-follow-your-excitement-formula/); [Channelya](https://channelya.com/authors/who-is-bashar/bashar-main-ideas/act-on-your-highest-excitement-bashar/)). The user's 168-item list carries its own provenance warning. Items 1–102 come from one transcript (the Hour of Power and five-forms-of-abundance video). Items **103–168 are the earlier assistant's extrapolations, not Bashar statements**. They are kept as theme evidence, and their source tags say so.

Across those sources, eight clusters recur. **Deservability and the struggle ethic** come first: things are "worthless… if you don't create suffering in order to get them", and "pain is more real than joy" (Blueprint p.10). Second is **determinism**, built around his central reframe "your present is not the result of your past" (Blueprint p.127). Third is an **external locus**: rescuers, wiser authorities, "seeing is believing" (Blueprint p.4, p.7, p.86–87). Fourth, **circumstances carry built-in negative meaning**, which he counters with "no circumstance has built-in meaning" and the missed-train example (Blueprint p.54, p.98–99). Fifth, **what I love can't pay**, "I have to struggle in order to make a living" (Blueprint p.155–156). Sixth, **outcome insistence**, which is the most consistently named pattern online ([Actualized.org](https://www.actualized.org/forum/topic/55489-bashar-youre-making-it-too-complicated/)). Seventh, **over-analysis**: "you're making it too complicated" (same source). Eighth, **fear read as danger or weakness**, from *The Hidden Truth about Fear* ([Channelya](https://channelya.com/authors/who-is-bashar/bashar-main-ideas/act-on-your-highest-excitement-bashar/)). The tree already covers clusters one, five (in part) and six well, through G1, G8, T7, A6, O1–O4 and V5-joy-130. Clusters two, three, four and seven are where most new surface beliefs land.

Bashar's own "counter-definitions" are better material for *preferred* beliefs than for limiting ones. Examples are doubt as "100% total conviction in a negative reality" (Blueprint p.160), anxiety as excitement "with judgment placed upon it" (p.174–175), a block as information "in an unexpected package" (p.137) and limbo as a point of power (p.116–121). The questioners' own words make the best surface beliefs, because they are already first-person and secular: "I feel so incompetent" (p.169), "I would probably just dance for free, but…" (p.159). Several of Bashar's claims overreach and must stay at **Model** or **Speculative** in the book's epistemic scheme. These include the assertion that "physicists… are beginning to realize that your thoughts… are the actual driving… force" (p.8), "100% guaranteed" (p.89), and "you can only get a positive reality back" (p.162). The secular core underneath them is reappraisal and expectancy effects. *Masters* contributes one mechanism worth keeping as an app prompt, a diagnostic question: "What would I have to believe is true about myself… in order to feel, think, behave the way I do?" (Masters ch.8). It also warns that a belief can "hide" behind easier ones, which yields the surface belief "I've already dealt with that, so it can't be what's driving this" (Masters ch.5).

## Koch attacks the equation that effort equals results

Koch's belief content is concentrated in two books. *Unreasonable Success* (2020) covers self-belief, expectations, setbacks and intuition. *The 80/20 Principle* (1997) and *Living the 80/20 Way* (2004) cover effort, busyness, time, happiness, relationships and money ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Koch)). He explicitly rejects four ideas: that we have already done all we can to improve our lives, that seeking pleasure is selfish or lazy, that time is scarce, and that **doing more is the only way to get more** ([Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/what-is-the-key-to-success/)). He argues that busy is not productive, that more hours do not mean more output, and that trying to please everyone pleases no one ([Blinkist](https://www.blinkist.com/en/books/living-the-80-slash-20-way-en)). He also argues that rewards are concentrated rather than proportional to effort ([Books That Slay](https://booksthatslay.com/the-80-20-principle-summary-and-analysis/)). In *Unreasonable Success*, self-belief is **built, not innate**. For 17 of the 20 people he studied, heritage and background were irrelevant to it ([Butler-Bowdon](https://tombutlerbowdon.substack.com/p/the-success-x-factor-few-will-talk)). The book also challenges modest goals, following proven routes and judgment by analysis alone ([SoBrief](https://sobrief-staging.onrender.com/books/unreasonable-success-and-how-to-achieve-it)). *Superconnect* adds that opportunity comes more from weak links than from close friends ([Management Today](https://www.managementtoday.co.uk/book-review-superconnect-richard-koch-greg-lockwood/article/1004496)).

Most of Koch's challenged beliefs land in existing cores. Examples: "I should only set goals I'm sure I can reach" goes to `small`, "Self-confidence is something you're born with" to `helpless`, and "I need everyone to like me" to `judged`. Koch also confirms the Bashar material from an independent, secular direction: both reject "what I love can't pay" and "seeing is believing", and both reject worry as a protective strategy ([Boing Boing interview](https://boingboing.net/2011/05/12/8020.html)). His epistemic status needs care. Pareto-type distributions are well documented, but "80/20" is a heuristic. His success claims come from a hand-picked sample of famous people, so they carry **survivorship bias** and belong at **Model/Plausible**. Shortform notes that people in poverty may lack the bandwidth to "work less" ([Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/what-is-the-key-to-success/)). For that reason, every Koch-derived preferred belief is worded with "can" and "often", never as a promise. His prescriptive advice was left out: concentrated investing, going self-employed early, and the physics "laws" of *Beyond the 80/20 Principle* (excluded under the Grondwet's quantum rule).

## Five cores pass the MECE test; `struggle` does not

The rule was strict: a new core only when a theme truly cannot fit any of the 32. Each proposal was checked on two things: does it protect against something the existing cores don't, and does it need a different exercise?

| Proposal | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| `fixed` + `fused` | **Merge into `fused`** (root Control) | Both name the same stance: treating your own perception, thoughts or feelings as reality. "Fixed" stresses perception, "fused" stresses inner experience. One core with two rules covers both without splitting emotional reasoning across cores. |
| `certain` | **Adopt** (Control) | `control` manages outcomes by *doing*, `outcome` judges results *afterwards*, `change` fears the *new*. None holds the demand to *know, understand or prove first*. This is the largest cluster in the 168 list. |
| `grief` | **Adopt** (Belonging) | `leave2` anticipates a loss, `pay` punishes guilt, `without` locates identity in the other person. Only `grief` holds pain as loyalty after a loss that has *already happened*. |
| `owe` | **Adopt** (Enjoyment) | `deserve` says I'm not *worthy* of good things. `owe` says good things *create a debt*. The protective motive (avoiding obligation and dependence) and the exercise (receive with only "thank you") are both different. It is the weakest of the five. |
| `grind` | **Adopt** (Control) | `earn` ties *worth* to output, `helpless` says effort is useless, `control` holds things together. Only `grind` makes the *causal* claim that results scale with input. |
| `struggle` | **Do not adopt** | Split three ways (see below). |

The constructs behind the four psychological cores are measured and named. That earns the cores a **Plausible** tag in the book's convention, while the tree itself stays **Model**. `certain` matches need for cognitive closure, the desire for definite knowledge, with an "urgency" tendency (seize an answer fast) and a "permanence" tendency (keep it) ([Kruglanski & Webster 1996](https://terpconnect.umd.edu/~hannahk/NFC-KW96.html)). It also matches intolerance of uncertainty, which has been proposed, though not settled, as possibly *the* fundamental fear ([Carleton 2016](https://uregina.scholaris.ca/items/5d1d1960-6e1a-4cfd-8619-b9c46ef59ff9)). `fused` matches ACT's cognitive fusion, behaviour "overly regulated and influenced by cognition", which has a validated 7-item questionnaire ([Gillanders et al. 2014](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/15143836/Gillanders_et_al_2014.pdf)). `grief` matches the "Cherish Grief" subscale of the Grief Cognitions Questionnaire, for example "As long as I mourn I maintain the bond". The same instrument's "Appropriateness of Grief" subscale supplies one more surface belief ([Frontiers 2021, reporting Boelen & Lensvelt-Mulders](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7848142)). `owe` matches indebtedness research: guilt and felt obligation raised the odds of *rejecting* help, while gratitude went with accepting it ([Gao et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10762097)).

### Struggle, grind and earn: one theme with three distinct claims

"Anything worthwhile has to be hard" is Bashar's most repeated limiting belief (Blueprint p.10, 49, 89–90, 120, 155–156, 163, 230). Read closely, though, its instances make three different claims. **Worth claims** say ease disqualifies the result: "If it comes easily, it doesn't really count" (A6), "Success I didn't struggle for isn't really mine" (G8), and the new "Money only counts if I worked hard for it". These are about credit and value, which is what `earn` owns. **Permission claims** say I may only enjoy things after hardship: "Pleasure has to be earned through hardship first" (G5), and G1/G9. These stay in `joy`, `deserve` and `pay`. **Efficacy claims** say hardness and volume are how results happen: "Earning a living has to be a struggle", "If something sounds simple, it can't really work", "Changing a habit is always a hard, uphill fight", "Life is a struggle; anything worth having has to be fought for". These are the same causal claim Koch attacks ("more hours always means more results"). A `struggle` core would either duplicate `grind` or force A6, G8, T7 and G5 to be moved out of the cores where their worth and permission motives belong. So `grind` takes the efficacy instances, and no existing id moves. The test for future items: if the belief says hard work makes the result *count*, it goes to `earn`. If it says hard work *produces* the result, it goes to `grind`. If it says I'm only *allowed* to enjoy something after hardship, it goes to `deserve` or `joy`.

Housekeeping follows from the decisions. The notes recommend moving four existing items to `certain`: **O4** "I have to know how this ends before I can start", **O9** "I need a guarantee before I'm willing to commit", **U4** "Uncertainty means something is wrong" and **U8** "If I don't know the next step, I shouldn't take this one". They also recommend moving **V5-discomfort-14** "Anxiety means something bad is about to happen", a textbook case of emotional reasoning, to `fused`. The `outcome` core's second rule ("I should know how it ends before I start") now duplicates `certain` and should be reworded, for example to "If it went differently, it was wasted." Two optional moves floated in the notes were left out on purpose: J8 (inherited worldview) stays in `fit`, because its motive is belonging, and U10 stays in `outcome`. N-control-2 ("I need to know exactly where this relationship is going") arguably belongs in `certain`, but no note recommended the move. That is an editor's call.

## 246 proposals collapse to 170 distinct beliefs

De-duplication ran at three levels: against the 351 existing beliefs and each core's rules, across the five notes, and within each note. The same idea often appeared four times. For example, "My past decides who I can be now" came from the 168 list, Blueprint p.127, *Masters* ch.12 and an online thread, and is now one `helpless` item. "Push harder when it isn't working" appeared under `control` in two notes and under `grind` in Koch's. It now sits once, under `grind`. Event-meaning beliefs ("it can only be bad news", "nothing good can come of it", "can only lead to more bad", "just bad luck") collapse into one `outcome` item. Keeping the cascade version under `bad` would have sat too close to S10. Several proposals were dropped as rewordings of an existing core or rule. "My everyday choices don't affect anything" is the `meaning` core's own text, "I need someone more powerful to sort it out" is the `cope` rule, and "stuck for good" is M3. "A forecast is a fixed sentence" was too close to M5, and "a secure job is the only responsible choice" too close to N-small-1. Two cross-core collisions were settled by moving the newcomer. "Feeling afraid means I'm weak" goes under `enough`, beside V5-enough-58, not `flawed`. "Feeling afraid means I shouldn't do it" merges with "resistance means it's not for me" into one `fused` item. A script confirmed no exact text duplicates within the 170 or against the database.

The new beliefs spread unevenly, and the imbalance is informative. `helpless` takes **17**, `outside` and `grind` 12 each, and `enough` and `certain` 10 each. Seven cores get one or none (`unlovable`, `leave`, `leave2` and `usedby` get nothing new). By area, **self has 53** and work 36, while body (4), rest (4) and family (3) stay thin. Neither Bashar nor Koch says much about bodies or families, which matches the transcript's topics. The `helpless` load is the warning sign. It now mixes "my past decides" with "change takes years", "confidence is innate" and "people don't change". If it keeps growing, it could later split into an agency core and a fixedness core. For now the items remain MECE under the core's protective motive (sparing yourself the effort and risk of trying). The closest remaining judgement calls are "If I feel incompetent, I must be" (`fused`) vs V5-enough-58; "Without a steady paycheck" vs N-unsafe-4; "Making my own happiness a priority is selfish" vs D9; and "If no one else is doing it, it's probably a bad idea" vs AU7.

## Safety lines the app must hold

Several new beliefs touch places where a cheerful reframe can do harm. **Grief**: all four `grief` items and `without` "If I lose someone, I lose part of who I am" should route to gentle, non-pushy copy, with no "let go" pressure. They need the coach's safety limits and a pointer to professional help when grief is prolonged or impairing. The preferred belief "I can keep the bond and let the pain soften" is deliberately not "move on". **Victim-blame**: Bashar's "the victim creates victimhood" (Blueprint p.95–96, p.109) was rejected as a frame. "When bad things happen to me, it's what I deserve", "Taking responsibility means admitting it's all my fault" and "If I own my part… I'm letting them off the hook" are written to separate responsibility from blame. They must never be offered about abuse, assault or crime. **Depression signals**: "Nothing really excites me", "This pain, or this low mood, will never lift" and "If I feel stuck or keep struggling, it's all my own failing" can reflect anhedonia or depressive self-blame, so the app should signpost help there instead of coaching harder. **Medical**: "Once a doctor names it, my story is written" covers meaning and agency only, never cure. Bashar's "fear causes the pain" (Blueprint p.278) and "transformation symptoms" such as palpitations and fainting (p.129–140) were rejected, because they need medical assessment. **Money**: "Without a steady paycheck…" says "plan", not "quit". Bashar's "quit your job and trust" (p.156, p.179) must not be echoed. Koch's "work less" carries the bandwidth caveat. **Scrupulosity**: "In the end, I'll have to pay for everything I did wrong" stays secular and non-theological. Everything metaphysical stays out as fact: higher self, vibration, parallel Earths, survival of consciousness, "what you put out comes back" (Masters ch.7, 11, 12).

## Conclusion

The most useful finding is structural, not numerical. The 32 cores of v0.5 are all *content* beliefs, about worth, people, danger, outcomes and joy. Bashar's sharpest idea, that a belief is a definition you can examine, has no home until `certain` and `fused` exist, because those two cores describe the beliefs that stop examination itself. They deserve prominent screens and their own exercises: one small try instead of more analysis for `certain`, and "is this a thought or a fact?" for `fused`. Koch's contribution is the counterweight the book needs. A secular, business-literate source attacks the same struggle ethic Bashar does, from evidence about leverage instead of from metaphysics. That lets the book teach "follow the pull without grinding" without asking the sceptic to accept a channelled source.

Going forward, the main risk is not missing beliefs but drift. `helpless` and the self area are absorbing too much. Body, rest and family need sources beyond these two authors (for example the earlier tree research), not more extrapolation from the transcript. The struggle–grind–earn test (does hard work make the result *count*, *produce* it, or *permit* enjoying it?) should become a written rule in the consistency ledger, so later additions stay MECE.
